Roof Leak Water Damage Help in Florence

Where you see the stain and where the rain gets in are usually two different places. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. We find the full wet area and dry it out.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. Our job is the inside of the house: pulling out soaked insulation, drying the framing and drywall, treating for mold and putting the ceiling back together. Should the leak still be active, our crew can cover that section with a tarp for now, or time our work with the roofing contractor you pick so nothing gets sealed over an open leak.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsWater coming through a ceiling fan, a light or close to an outlet means that circuit should be turned off at the panel, and the fixture should not be touched.
  2. Contain the WaterPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Stay Off the RoofClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. From indoors, mark where the water enters, and let professionals handle everything else.
  5. Photograph the DamageBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.

Our Process

Our Roof Leak Water Damage Process

1

Map Where Water Went

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks wall cavities, ceilings and the attic and charts the whole route the water took, even where the room below shows nothing.

2

Tarp and Protect

If the roof is still open to the weather, we can install a temporary tarp or coordinate with your roofer. Anything sitting beneath the damage, including the flooring, gets protected too.

3

Tear Out and Dry

We pull out insulation and drywall that cannot be saved, then run air movers and dehumidifiers in the attic and the rooms below, checking moisture readings until the framing is dry.

4

Sanitize and Repair

An antimicrobial product goes on any open wood that needs it. After the roofer has finished, new insulation goes in, fresh drywall is hung and finished, and the ceiling is primed and painted to match.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Missing or broken shingles, often after high wind, with bare underlayment beneath.
  • Gaps at chimneys, vents and sidewalls where the flashing has separated or rusted through.
  • Rubber boots around plumbing vent pipes that split after years in the sun.
  • Leaves and grit collecting where two roof slopes meet, damming rain until it seeps through.
  • Clogged gutters that back water up under the shingles at the roof edge.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Roof Framing

Rafters, trusses and decking that get wet again and again begin to darken, swell and soften. Decay here means less strength in what carries the roof.

Hidden Attic Mold

Mold does well in an attic that is warm, dark and full of damp wood and drywall paper. Within 24 to 48 hours it can start growing, then move across the bottom of the roof deck.

Insulation in the Attic

Once soaked, insulation flattens, loses its value and keeps the drywall under it damp for days. Fiberglass can sometimes be dried. Removal is the usual answer for wet cellulose.

Ceiling Drywall

A ceiling panel left damp will discolor, bow and weaken. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.
